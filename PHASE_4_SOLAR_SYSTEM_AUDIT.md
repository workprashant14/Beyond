## 2. Verified this turn

* **Planet positions vs an independent ephemeris.** Check in `tests/timescales.test.ts`: heliocentric ecliptic J2000 positions of all eight planets on five dates (1900, 1950, 2000, 2025-11-21, 2050) compared with astropy's built-in ephemeris (ERFA `epv00` for Earth, `plan94` for the others; fixture `tests/fixtures/planets-oracle.json`, regenerated by `tests/helpers/make_planet_oracle.py`). Worst direction difference **9.2 arcminutes** (Saturn), Mercury/Venus/Earth/Mars at or below about 1 arcminute, distances within 0.15%. Both sources are approximations, so this shows there are no transcription or algorithm errors (those would be degrees); it does **not** show JPL-DE accuracy. JPL's own stated error for this table over 1800 to 2050 is 15 arcsec (Mercury) up to 400 (Jupiter) and 600 (Saturn) arcsec.
* **Time scales** (`utils/timeScales.ts`, new): TT − UTC matches ERFA at 13 sampled dates (leap-second table 1972 to 2017); TDB − TT within 50 µs.

## 3. Inaccurate or misleading assumptions found

## 5. Data that cannot be produced from this sandbox

The sandbox cannot reach JPL (ssd.jpl.nasa.gov, ssd-api.jpl.nasa.gov, Horizons). Therefore these are **not built and not verified here**: JPL satellite mean elements for all moons, Horizons state vectors, SBDB asteroid/comet/TNO records. Builders for small bodies exist (`npm run build:smallbodies`); a moon-elements builder and a dwarf-planet/TNO selection still have to be written and then run by you. I will not write a parser for a page layout I cannot read; I will ask you to paste a small sample of the real response first.
